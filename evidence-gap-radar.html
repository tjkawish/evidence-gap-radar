    How the radar decides what's a gap

    Counts. Each cell is the number of PubMed records matching topic AND row AND column, plus the humans and year filters if they're on. Row and column totals use the topic with one axis only.

    Expected. If the topic's literature were spread evenly, a cell would hold row total × column total ÷ topic total papers. That's the same independence model as a chi-square table. Say cohort designs are 18% of youth mental health papers worldwide. Then about 18% of Pakistan's papers on it should be cohorts too.

    Gap score. z = (observed − expected) ÷ √expected, a Poisson z-score. A cell with z ≤ −2 gets a dashed border. A cell with no papers is marked in orange whenever at least one paper was expected.

    Read with care. These are search hits, not screened studies. MeSH indexing lags by months, so the newest papers carry only title and abstract terms. A low count can also mean the search string misses that field's vocabulary. Treat a flagged cell as a lead worth a proper scoping search, not a finding in itself.

    Counts and article details come from PubMed (US National Library of Medicine), searched with your own PubMed connector. The beyond-PubMed check uses your Paperguide connector; the Google Scholar button opens a normal Scholar search. Cite articles by their DOIs.
